4. VERDICT — did base-loop injection work?
  Yes, with a caveat that matters.
  Supported: every pair among the five went from ~0 to 0.08-0.36; the family went 0.05 -> 0.19, one pair short of target. The pipeline sees the loop
  when the text carries it. Delta papers were the bottleneck, not section choice (Phase 20) and not the ontology.
  Residual (the 0.01 gap): Rainbow store@other (typing), TD3 compressing store/sample/select into three update@ ops (extractor summarised the
  injected pseudocode instead of listing it). Both are noise, not structure.
  Caveat: the score is partly by construction. Prepending DQN's loop to DDQN then measuring DDQN x DQN measures text we inserted. Lexical 0.63 is
  close to DQN-vs-itself. What the test legitimately shows: (a) the extractor recovers the loop from pseudocode in one pass, (b) the delta papers'
  own text does not carry it, (c) after injection the delta signal is mostly lost, so injected records are "base + one op". A record that is base
  loop plus delta needs both kept: the fair design is two records per delta paper (base-inherited ops flagged, delta ops own) or a union at compare time.

ANSWERS
  Q1 replay-off-policy >0.20?          0.19. No, by one pair. Four-fold rise from 0.05.
  Q2 which pairs rose most?            DDQN x Rainbow 0.00->0.33 (both DQN-based), DDPG x DDQN 0.09->0.20, DDPG x Rainbow 0.00->0.20, TD3 x DDQN/Rainbow 0.00->0.17.
                                       DQN x DDPG stayed 0.36 (both already had the loop). DDPG x TD3 only 0.10 — TD3 lost the loop verbs to "update".
  Q3 is delta-paper the true bottleneck?  Yes for RL. It explains 0.05 -> 0.19; typing noise explains the rest. It does not apply to jepa / pc-learning /
                                       on-policy, whose members each restate their loop — which is why those families cohered without help.
